Count in Lingala

The six rules of Lingala numeral formation. Print and keep.

  1. Units (0–9)

    Memorize the nine base words. Everything else is built from these.

    0eloko te
    1moko
    2mibale
    3misato
    4minei
    5mitano
    6motoba
    7nsambo
    8mwambe
    9libwa

  2. Ten & teens (10–19)

    Ten is zomi. For 11–19, say zomi na <unit>.

    12 = zomi na mibale · 17 = zomi na nsambo

  3. Tens (20–99)

    Multiples of ten use ntuku <count>. Compounds add na <unit>.

    20 = ntuku mibale · 47 = ntuku minei na nsambo · 99 = ntuku libwa na libwa

  4. Hundreds (100–999)

    100 alone is bare nkama. Otherwise nkama <count>, plus na <rest> for remainders. When h=1 and there's a remainder, you must say nkama moko.

    100 = nkama · 247 = nkama mibale na ntuku minei na nsambo · 105 = nkama moko na mitano

  5. Thousands (1,000–999,999)

    1000 alone is bare nkoto. Otherwise nkoto <count>, plus na <rest> for remainders.

    1000 = nkoto · 1200 = nkoto moko na nkama mibale · 5000 = nkoto mitano

  6. Millions (1,000,000+)

    Three structural blocks separated by commas, not na: million <count>, then nkoto <count>, then under-1000. Inside each block, na joins parts.

    1,000,000 = million moko
    1,123,456 = million moko, nkoto nkama moko na ntuku mibale na misato, na nkama minei na ntuku mitano na motoba
